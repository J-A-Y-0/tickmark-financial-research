## Summary

Harbor Point initiates coverage of Uber Technologies with an Overweight house rating and a fictional 12-month price target of $92. Our thesis rests on three points: Uber has become a profitable platform at scale, Mobility and Delivery share one consumer base, and advertising adds high-margin revenue on top of existing traffic.

## What the 10-K shows

In fiscal 2024 Uber reported revenue of $44.0 billion, up 18% year over year, and Gross Bookings of $162.8 billion. Adjusted EBITDA reached $6.5 billion and free cash flow was $6.9 billion. Net income of $9.9 billion was inflated by a one-time $6.4 billion benefit from releasing a tax valuation allowance, so we do not use reported net income in our valuation.

## House view

We model Gross Bookings growth of 15% in 2025 and 13% in 2026. We expect Adjusted EBITDA as a percentage of Gross Bookings to expand from 4.0% in 2024 to 4.6% in 2026, driven by Delivery margin gains and advertising. Our base case values Uber at 22x 2026 estimated free cash flow.

## Key risks

- Driver classification: a ruling that treats drivers as employees in a large market would raise costs.
- Autonomous vehicles: robotaxi partners could become competitors instead of supply.
- Insurance costs in the United States continue to pressure Mobility pricing.

## Monitoring

We will revisit the rating if Gross Bookings growth falls below 10% for two consecutive quarters.
